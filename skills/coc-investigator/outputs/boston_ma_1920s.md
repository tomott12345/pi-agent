# Boston, MA (1920s) – Keeper’s Dossier

## 📍 SECTION 1: THE LOCATION

Boston in the early 1920s is a city of stark contrasts. The harbor smells of salt, fish, and the occasional whiff of fermenting molasses that still lingers from the 1919 **Boston Molasses Disaster** (Wikipedia: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Molasses_Disaster>). Cobblestone streets wind between the grand, Federal‑style mansions of the Brahmin elite and the cramped tenements of Irish and Italian immigrants in South Boston. Streetcars clatter over the tracks that snake past the **Custom House Tower**, newly completed in 1915, its illuminated spire a beacon for ships entering the harbor.

The atmosphere is foggy and damp, the air heavy with the smell of the sea and the occasional whiff of coal smoke. The **Boston Common** and **Public Garden** provide a manicured green oasis, yet locals whisper of strange lights flickering over the Common at night. The **Old State House** — the 1713 brick building that witnessed the Boston Massacre — stands under a veil of legend; staff report a “Lady in White” appearing on the balcony before tragedies. The city’s underworld thrives in the **Scollay Square** district, where speakeasies and gambling dens operate under the watch of a corrupt police force led by Chief Inspector **Thomas Byrnes** (Wikipedia: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Police_Department>).

Beneath the bustling surface, old cellars and tunnels — some dating to the colonial era — create a labyrinthine network that could hide secrets far older than the city itself.

## 🐙 SECTION 5: MYTHOS CONNECTIONS

- **Primary Threat:** **Nyarlathotep** (the Crawling Chaos) – he manipulates Boston’s elite through the **Ordo Templi Orientis** chapter that meets in the Athenæum’s hidden vault. He seeks to open a permanent portal in the Old State House basement, allowing his influence to seep into the city’s mortal affairs.  

- **Local Cult:** **The Boston O.T.O. Circle** – a loosely organized group of wealthy Brahmins, occult scholars, and corrupt police officials. They meet in the basement of the Old State House, believing they are summoning “higher intelligences” to grant them power. In reality, they are unwittingly facilitating Nyarlathotep’s plan.  

- **Physical Evidence of Mythos Activity:**  
  1. **Architectural Anomaly:** The east wing of the Old State House contains a set of stairs that appear to loop back on themselves; the angles are impossible in Euclidean space (non‑Euclidean). Investigators may discover a hidden door behind a false wall.  
  2. **Biological Anomaly:** In the basement of the Boston Public Library, a patch of **glowing, salt‑scented mold** grows on the floor near a sealed archive. The mold emits a faint bioluminescence and seems to react to sound.  
  3. **Documentary Anomaly:** A 1921 *Boston Herald* article (Wikipedia) reports a “strange luminous fog” over the harbor on the night of the Molasses Disaster, contradicting the official explanation of “gas leakage”. The fog was later identified by investigators as a **manifestation of a Deep One** entity.

## 🎭 SECTION 6: SCENARIO HOOKS

### 1. Hook Name: *The Molasses Veil*
- **The Call:** A frantic telegram arrives from **Dr. Lionel Hargreaves**, a chemist at the Purity Distilling Company, stating he has uncovered a “dark secret” in the molasses tank and fears for his life.  
- **Surface Mystery:** The tank is being drained for cleaning; city officials plan a public memorial.  
- **The Truth:** The tank once housed a ritual mixture that summoned a **Deep One** spawn; the disaster was a failed attempt to bind the creature. The black molasses is still alive and slowly spreading.  
- **Point of No Return:** When investigators open the tank and witness the sentient, black ooze moving like a living thing, they must decide whether to seal it or risk contaminating the city.  
- **Potential Endings:**  
  - *Good*: Seal the tank, prevent further spread, rescue Hargreaves.  
  - *Bad*: The spawn escapes, infecting the harbor and causing a city‑wide panic.  
  - *Worse*: The spawn merges with the molasses, creating a **new hybrid entity** that threatens the entire New England region.  

### 2. Hook Name: *The White Lady’s Warning*
- **The Call:** Night watchman **Thomas “Tommy” Doyle** contacts the investigators, claiming he saw the “Lady in White” on the Old State House balcony and heard a chant in an unknown tongue.  
- **Surface Mystery:** The Old State House is a historic museum; the watchman is uneasy but wants to keep his job.  
- **The Truth:** The Lady is a bound servant of **Nyarlathotep**, attempting to warn that a portal will open during the upcoming town council meeting, allowing Nyarlathotep’s influence to flood the city.  
- **Point of No Return:** Attending the council meeting as the portal begins to manifest (the walls seem to ripple, shadows deepen).  
- **Potential Endings:**  
  - *Good*: Perform a sealing ritual (using the BSPR files) before the portal fully opens.  
  - *Bad*: The portal opens, and Nyarlathotep’s influence drives the city’s leaders mad.  
  - *Worse*: The investigators become pawns of Nyarlathotep, gaining forbidden knowledge but losing their sanity.  

### 3. Hook Name: *The Cabot Curse*
- **The Call:** **Eleanor Cabot**, a descendant of the Cabot family, hires the investigators to look into a series of bizarre suicides among her relatives, fearing a family curse.  
- **Surface Mystery:** The Cabot estate “Braeside” is in turmoil; family members die under strange circumstances (poison, falls, fire).  
- **The Truth:** The Cabots inadvertently opened a **gate** beneath the Charles River to **Cthulhu’s** sleeping city. The suicides are induced by a psychic wave from the Great Old One’s dream.  
- **Point of No Return:** Descending into the underground tunnels beneath Braeside and seeing the **Great Old One**’s massive, sleeping form.  
- **Potential Endings:**  
  - *Good*: Close the gate using the sigil from the Cabot diary and a sacrifice.  
  - *Bad*: The gate stays open; Cthulhu begins to stir, threatening global catastrophe.  
  - *Worse*: The investigators are corrupted, becoming cultists who serve Cthulhu, forever bound to the underwater city.

## 📋 KEEPER’S NOTES

- **Recommended Investigator Archetypes:**  
  - A **Boston Brahmin** with social connections to the O.T.O. Circle.  
  - A **Prohibition‑era police officer** disillusioned with corruption.  
  - A **journalist** from the *Boston Herald* who has witnessed the “luminous fog”.  
  - A **Harvard professor of folklore** familiar with colonial‑era occult practices.  

- **Tone:** Noir‑style investigation with cosmic horror undertones; the city’s historic grandeur masks unspeakable dread.  

- **Session Count:** One‑shot (2–3 hours) for a quick plunge, or 2–3 sessions for deeper research, multiple locations, and the final ritual.  

- **Connections to Other Locations:**  
  - **Salem, MA** – Witch trial archives may hold clues about the “Cult of the New England”.  
  - **Cambridge, MA** – Harvard’s libraries and the **Miskatonic University** (fictional) could provide forbidden tomes.  
  - **Portsmouth, NH** – Naval base records may reveal ship‑based encounters with Deep Ones.  
  - **Maine Coast** – Known Deep One settlements; a possible escape route for the creature from the molasses tank.  

*All facts marked with Wikipedia links are drawn from the corresponding articles. Mythos interpretations are speculative and intended for use in a Call of Cthulhu campaign.*
